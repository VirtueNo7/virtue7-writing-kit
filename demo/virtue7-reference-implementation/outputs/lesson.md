# Pride → Humility: Face the Truth

## Learning outcome

The learner can distinguish confidence from defensive pride, explain how correction prevents repeated harm, and choose one safe, proportionate act of humility.

## Scenario

A team member sends the wrong file, then insists the instructions were unclear even after seeing the original message. The error can still be repaired, but the delay grows while the person protects an image of competence.

## Compare

What does protecting the image create next? What becomes possible when the person says, “I sent the wrong file. I will correct it now”?

## Reflection

Think of a recent correction you resisted. What felt threatened: your safety, your dignity, or your image? These are not the same problem and may require different responses.

## Practice

Choose one small correction that is safe to make within twenty-four hours. The practice is voluntary and must not be used to pressure another person into confession or submission.

## Understanding check

Explain why humility can require courage and why accepting mistreatment is not the same as humility.
