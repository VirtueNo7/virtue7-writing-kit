# The Crown in the Puddle

Milo the magpie found a bright paper crown beside the market. He set it between his black feathers and announced, “A king never makes mistakes.”

That afternoon, Milo promised to carry three red berries to the nest. He flew too fast, bumped a branch, and dropped every berry into a muddy puddle.

Tess the tortoise looked up. “The branch was low,” she said. “Next time, fly around it.”

“I did fly around it,” Milo replied, although the mud was still dripping from his tail.

He searched for new berries. Again he flew too fast. Again the branch knocked them down. His paper crown slipped over one eye.

“The crown cannot see for you,” said Tess.

Milo wanted to blame the wind, the branch, and the berries for being unusually bouncy. Instead, he looked at the puddle. Six red berries floated there, all because he had protected his boast instead of correcting his flight.

“I was wrong,” he said. The words felt heavy for one breath and light for every breath after it.

Milo folded the crown into a little map. Tess drew the low branch on it. On his third flight, Milo slowed down, turned early, and brought the berries home.

A crown can cover an eye. The truth can show the way.
