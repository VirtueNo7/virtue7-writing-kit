## Draft artifact

# The Bridge That Learned to Bend

At the edge of a small blue town stood a bridge named Brindle, stiff as a ruler and twice as proud.

“Straight is strong,” Brindle would sing. “Straight all day is the proper way.”

Then the rain came tap-tap-tapping.

It filled the creek with a tumble and roar, and the little stones below began to scoot, skitter, and spin.

“Hold tight!” cried the reeds.

“I always hold tight,” said Brindle, locking every board and bolt.

The water pushed. Brindle pushed back.

The water pushed harder.

Brindle gave a tiny creak.

Then a larger creeeak.

On the bank, an old willow lowered one green branch. “Strong things do not always stay still,” she said. “Sometimes they share the shove.”

Brindle did not like the sound of that. Sharing was for sandwiches and songs, not bridges.

But the creek rose higher, and Brindle felt the force gather against one stubborn spot beneath the middle rail.

So Brindle tried something new.

He let one joint loosen a whisker. Another shifted a wink. The beams passed the pressure from plank to post, from post to stone, and from stone into the waiting ground.

Bendle, lendle, pass it along.

The bridge did not break. It moved just enough to remain whole.

By morning, the creek had shrunk to a silver ribbon. The townspeople crossed with baskets, boots, and one sleepy goat who stopped halfway to nibble a loose thread from a scarf.

Brindle hummed beneath them.

“Straight can be strong,” he sang, “but sharing the strain keeps strength in the frame.”

The willow rustled. The reeds applauded. Even the creek gave a soft little clap against the stones.

From then on, whenever the wind blew hard or the water ran wild, Brindle did not freeze himself into fear. He listened, shifted, shared, and held.

Bendle, lendle, pass it along.

And that became the bridge’s proper way.
